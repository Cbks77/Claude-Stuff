# Piece of Velvet CIC — Setup & Funding Guide

**Mission:** A Community Interest Company in St Paul's / Montpelier, Bristol (BS2/BS6),
training young people affected by or at risk of knife crime, giving them Level 2 and
Level 3 Food Safety qualifications and real bakery work experience, and routing them into
jobs in Bristol's hospitality sector.

*Researched July 2026. Always confirm fees/deadlines on the linked pages before applying.*

---

## 1. What a CIC is, and why it fits

A CIC is a normal limited company with two extras: an **asset lock** (assets and profits
must serve the community purpose) and a **community interest statement** approved by the
CIC Regulator. It can trade (sell cakes, run the bakery arm), pay directors, and apply for
most grants aimed at community organisations.

**Recommendation: CIC limited by guarantee.** Almost every funder listed below accepts
CICs, but several (Lottery, many trusts) prefer or require limited-by-guarantee. Only
choose limited-by-shares if you plan to raise private investment — and note extra rules on
profit distribution apply.

## 2. Registering the CIC — step by step

1. **Choose a name** — e.g. "Piece of Velvet CIC". Check availability on the
   [Companies House register](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/).
2. **Recruit directors** — you need at least 2 (ideally 3) **unrelated** board members.
   This is also a hard eligibility rule for National Lottery Awards for All. Strong picks:
   someone from a partner bakery, a youth worker, someone with lived experience.
3. **Write the CIC36 community interest statement** — describes (a) the community you
   serve and (b) how activities benefit them. Draft wording:
   > *"Piece of Velvet CIC will benefit young people aged 16–25 in the St Paul's,
   > Montpelier and inner-city Bristol area who have been affected by, or are at risk of
   > involvement in, knife crime and serious youth violence. We will deliver accredited
   > Level 2 and Level 3 Food Safety training, practical baking skills and supported work
   > placements with local bakeries, creating routes into employment in the hospitality
   > sector. Any surplus will be reinvested in training places, trainee wages and
   > equipment."*
   Every first director must sign section D of the CIC36.
4. **Adopt CIC model articles** — use the CIC Regulator's model articles for a company
   limited by guarantee ("large membership" version is the usual choice).
5. **File the application** — online via Companies House, uploading the CIC36 and
   articles. **Fee: £115 online / £139 postal (rates from 1 Feb 2026).** Postal takes up
   to 15 working days; online is faster. Directors now also go through Companies House
   identity verification.
6. **After approval:** open a **bank account in the CIC's name** (funder requirement —
   Lloyds, Co-op, Unity Trust and CAF all do community accounts), register with HMRC,
   get **employers' & public liability insurance**, and register as a **food business**
   with Bristol City Council (free, at least 28 days before operating).
7. **Ongoing:** file an annual CIC34 community interest report + accounts.

**Key official pages:**
- [Form CIC36 + guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/form-cic36-application-to-form-a-community-interest-company)
- [CIC step-by-step guides](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/community-interest-companies-forms-and-step-by-step-guides/cic-business-activities-forms-and-step-by-step-guidelines)
- [Companies House fees](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/companies-house-fees)

## 3. Safeguarding — non-negotiable before funders will pay

Because trainees are young and at risk, before delivery starts you need:
- A written **safeguarding policy** and a named safeguarding lead
- **Enhanced DBS checks** for staff/volunteers working with young people
- Risk assessments covering knife/equipment use in a kitchen environment (funders will
  ask specifically, given the cohort)
- Insurance that covers under-18s if you take 16–17 year olds

Voscur (below) can help you get these right — funders read them before deciding.

## 4. Delivering Level 2 & Level 3 Food Safety

Regulated qualifications (RQF) come from awarding bodies — **Highfield** is the biggest in
catering; RSPH and CIEH are alternatives. Two routes:

**Phase 1 — buy in training (start here):**
- [Highfield Level 2 Award in Food Safety for Catering](https://www.highfieldqualifications.com/products/qualifications/food-safety/level-2-for-catering)
  — 1 day + multiple-choice exam. Via local approved centres or
  [Highfield e-learning](https://www.highfieldelearning.com/online-courses/food-safety-level-3/);
  budget roughly **£20–£60 per learner** online, more classroom-based.
- [Highfield Level 3 Award in Food Safety for Catering](https://www.highfieldqualifications.com/products/qualifications/food-safety/level-3-catering)
  — supervisory level, typically 2–3 days; budget roughly **£250–£400 per learner**.
  Aim Level 3 at trainees showing team-leader potential.
- Bristol-area colleges and private centres run both; get 2–3 quotes for group bookings
  (a closed group of 8–12 trainees is usually far cheaper per head).

**Phase 2 — become a Highfield-approved training centre.** Once you have a qualified
trainer (Level 3 + training qualification), the CIC can deliver and certificate in-house.
Cuts per-learner cost massively and becomes a revenue stream (selling training to other
Bristol hospitality businesses funds free places for your young people).

**Wrap-around:** pair the qualification with 6–12 week paid/supported bakery placements,
employability support (CV, interviews) and a named mentor — this combination is exactly
what the violence-reduction funders below want to see.

### Government programmes to align with (not direct grants, but referral/funding routes)
- **[Turnaround Programme](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/turnaround-programme)** — £46m to March 2029, early intervention for young people on the edge of the justice system, delivered through Youth Justice Services: Bristol's YJS could refer trainees to you and fund places.
- **Young Futures Hubs / Youth Guarantee** — being rolled out in Bristol for 16–24s into
  work and training; position the CIC as a delivery partner.

### Free help
- **[Voscur](https://voscur.org/)** — Bristol's VCSE support body: free funding advice,
  governance and safeguarding help, social enterprise support and a funding-opportunities
  bulletin. **Contact them in week 1** — they know which Bristol pots are open right now.

## 7. Suggested first 90 days

1. **Weeks 1–2:** Confirm 2–3 directors; call Voscur; meet Herbert's (and confirm the
   second bakery) for letters of support.
2. **Weeks 2–4:** Draft CIC36 + adopt model articles; file online (£115). Start UnLtd
   eligibility checker (person-based, doesn't need the CIC registered).
3. **Weeks 4–8:** Bank account, insurance, safeguarding policy + DBS checks, food business
   registration. Write the core one-page project plan (cohort, numbers, outcomes, cost per
   trainee) — every funder gets a version of this.
4. **Weeks 8–12:** Submit Awards for All (£20k: tutor costs, Level 2/3 fees, trainee
   travel/kit, placement coordination) and John James Bristol Foundation. Introduce the
   CIC to the OPCC/VRP and Bristol Youth Justice Service. Get quotes from 3 Highfield
   centres for a group Level 2 booking so pilot training can start while grants process.

**Realistic first-year stack:** UnLtd £5–8k + Awards for All up to £20k + John James
£5–10k ≈ **£30–38k** — enough for a pilot cohort of 10–12 trainees with paid placements.
